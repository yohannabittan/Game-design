**Decisions from the designer (2026-10-12):**

- "What's missing for me is a lot of different cards that can synergise and are not just small incremental bonus bumps" (section K).

**What the roast changed from draft 1:**
- Each arena changes *how* you fight, not just the numbers.
- The crowd earns your gold.
- The market is folded into the draft, plus one tent stop.
- **Fame** and Brutus's **ludus** give lasting unlocks.
- You cripple *him* instead of carrying wounds yourself.
- The art goes on the fighters first.

## 1. Pitch

You are a nobody from the Pit with a reason to fight. Climb four arenas to the Grand Games before the Emperor:
- each arena asks a new kind of fight;
- the crowd pays for style;
- between runs, Old Brutus's school grows with every champion you fell.

Mastery is the same as v1: read the tell, read the feint, slip the blade into the gap.

## 2. Two loops

| | Lasts | Earned by | Spent on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Gold** | one run | the crowd's coins (C) | the draft's priced card and the armourer's tent (D) |
| **Fame** | forever | felling champions, and a first clear of an arena | the ludus (E): new options, not raw power |

**The rule that keeps it a skill game:** a lasting unlock widens your choices or adds flavour. It never makes the same fight easier by more than one small step.

## A. The ladder: four arenas, four kinds of fight

One run is one arena. The menu's Play opens an arena picker showing the arenas you have opened, plus the next one locked with its champion's silhouette. Felling an arena's champion opens the next arena for good.

| Arena | Bouts | Its twist | Built from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Pit** (Capua) | 5 | **The tight ring.** The walls stand closer, so backing off is short, wall play matters, and he can pin you. | the v1 walls, nearer |
| **The City arena** (Pompeii) | 5 | **Pairs.** From bout 3, a second foe waits at the edge. When the first is winded or felled, the second steps in, and felling both fells the bout. You fight one at a time, and his partner's taunts are his tells. | the v1 foe, swapped in |
| **The Colosseum** (Rome) | 5 | **Men and beasts.** Bouts 1 and 3 are men. Bouts 2 and 4 are beasts, a lion or a bear (rolled). A beast has no shield and no feints, just short-tell pounces and swipes; you read it by its crouch, not a weapon glow. The lion is quick and pounces; the bear is slow and mauls, and stands up to strike. The champion (bout 5) is a *bestiarius* who sends in his lion at half health, the City's pairs rule with a beast. | a new foe kind with two move sets |
| **The Grand Games** (Rome) | 3, then the Emperor's champion | **The Emperor's thumb.** After the final fell, the crowd meter decides the epilogue (C). | the crowd meter |

- **Each arena also tunes its foes,** in one per-arena table in TUNING: grade shift, feint rate and move set.
- **The arena's twist is the headline,** not the numbers.
- **The champions:**
  - the four v1 champions are now the Pit's champion pool;
  - each later arena has a pool of 2 champions with the same mechanics.
- **The rival** is remembered per arena.
- **The beast bout is the riskiest piece** (a new foe kind), so it is built and played first (H).

## B. Crippling him (replaces wounds on you)

- A gap hit on one of his limbs lames it **for the rest of the bout:**
  - **his sword arm:** his wind-ups take 20% longer;
  - **his shield arm:** his cover is slower to move;
  - **his legs:** his footwork and chase slow down.
- **v1 already has disabled parts.** This builds on them, so the effect reads at once: a limp, a dropped guard, a red mark.
- **At most one laming per limb.** Each is shown on his HUD slot as a small icon.
- **You carry nothing between bouts.** The v1 hearts and plates rules stand.

## C. The crowd

- **A crowd meter** sits small in the HUD, beside the BOUT slot, clear of both thumbs. It fills with style:

  | Style | Favour |
  | --- | --- |
  | a feint read | big |
  | a perfect block | medium |
  | a slot hit | medium |
  | a laming (B) | medium |
  | a bout with no hits taken | big |
  | a combo | small |

  It drains a little when you turtle (the existing turtle read) or run.
- **Coins in the sand.** At each favour step, the crowd throws coins: a cosmetic pop plus a coin sound. The gold is counted at the bout's end, shown as "+34 gold" on the grade card.
- **The Emperor's thumb** (the Grand Games only). After the final fell:
  - **a full meter:** thumbs up. The true epilogue: your reason pays off (E2);
  - **a middling meter:** a cold nod. A short epilogue, and the reason half resolved;
  - **a low meter:** thumbs down. The crowd jeers, and you are freed but forgotten.

  All three count as a win and a champion felled. Only the ending changes, so style matters at the top without making a win feel lost.
- **Glory** (the reason): the chant from v1 is driven by the meter.

## D. Gold: the draft and the armourer's tent

- **The draft deals three cards, as in v1, but one of them has a price.** It is drawn from a tier above the other two and costs gold. If you can't afford it, it shows its price greyed out. A gold card is a clear step stronger, so a stylish run earns a better run.
- **The armourer's tent, once per run after bout 3.** It offers three wares, rolled and seeded, each with a price:
  - a plate for one part;
  - a whetstone (+1 damage for the rest of the run);
  - a heart;
  - a respin or reroll token;
  - a better weapon swap (one weapon tier up, from your arena's pool).
  
  Skip is one tap. It shows only once a run, so it never slows the bout-to-bout pace.
- **Gold resets at the end of a run.** Any gold left over turns into a little fame (1 fame per 50 gold), so saving it is never wasted.

## E. Fame and the ludus

**Earning fame:**
- felling a champion: 10;
- a first clear of an arena: 25;
- a thumbs-up ending: 10;
- leftover gold: as in D.

**The ludus** is Brutus's school: a new menu page, a courtyard with five stations. Each unlock is one tap with a price and one line saying what it does:

| Station | Unlocks (examples, all in data) | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The armoury** | new weapons on your wheel: the trident and net (the existing net, now yours), and the curved sica | options |
| **The barracks** | new origins and backgrounds on the wheels, each with a perk: a gladiatrix from Sparta, a freed slave from Egypt, a chariot driver | options |
| **The training ground** | one starting-card slot (choose any card you have picked before); a 4th card in your first draft | small power, capped at these two |
| **The shrine** | new reasons with a face (e.g. *a promise*: to free a sister in the champion's house), each with its thread and epilogue | story |
| **The tailor** | crests, helmet plumes, shield paints, a cloak | cosmetic |

- **The Wall of Champions,** beside the pit wall: every fighter who cleared an arena, with their name, origin, weapon and arena.
- **A veteran.** From the Wall, you may start a run with a past champion instead of rolling. They keep their rolls, with +1 to one stat and a scar line, but only in an arena above the one they cleared. It is the lasting attachment the designer asked about, with a hard cap so it never snowballs.

## E2. Story

- **Each arena is a chapter.** Brutus has one opening line per arena, and your reason's thread climbs across the four:
  - **debt:** it reaches zero at the Grand Games;
  - **revenge:** the target is the Emperor's champion;
  - **freedom:** the Emperor himself hands over the wooden sword;
  - **glory:** Rome chants your name.
- **The thumb sets the tone of the ending** (C).
- **The v1 epilogue** becomes the Pit's ending.
- Text counts follow principle 11. Lines are written in the build and checked by the reviewer.

## F. Art (art chat, ADR-0015; Pack 15 written after this PRD is locked)

In order of what you look at:
1. **Gear on the fighters.** Painted helmets (one per gladiator type), shields, each weapon (gladius, spear, axe, dagger, trident and net, sica), and armour plates. Each is a sprite pinned to the existing skeleton (v1 already paints parts once into sprites), with the procedural look kept as the fallback. About 20 small images.
2. **The beast:** a lion, in 3 poses (crouch, pounce and swipe) on the same pinned-sprite rig. 3 images.
3. **Portraits:** Brutus, 8 champions (2 per later arena plus 2 new Pit faces), and the Emperor. 10 images.
4. **Backdrops:** one per arena behind the sand, kept soft so the fighters read. 4 images.

That is about 37 images, at about $6 to $10. Each step ships on its own, so step 1 can land before the portraits.

## K. The deck, rebuilt for builds (designer, 2026-10-12)

The v1 deck is mostly "+20% this". From now on, a card changes a **rule**. Cards work together through three things a bout produces, so picks combine into a build instead of adding up.

### K1. Three things cards make and spend

| Resource | Made by | Spent by | Shown as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Momentum** (0 to 5 pips, per bout) | perfect blocks, feint reads, a dodge in his tell, a laming | finishers (K3) | pips under your hearts |
| **Bleed** (stacks on him) | slot hits with a bleed card | ticks of damage, and payoffs that read his stacks | drops on his HUD slot |
| **The crowd** (C) | style | crowd cards, and the gold | the crowd meter |

### K2. Six families

Each card shows its family's icon. A family has an **enabler** that starts it, **payoffs** that reward it, and one **keystone**: a rare that bends a rule and costs you something.

| Family | Fantasy | Example cards (one line each) | Keystone (with its price) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Wall** | the shield is a weapon | **Riposte** (kept); **Thorns**: each block dents his weapon, and 3 dents disarm him, so he fights with fists; **Shield Shove**: a perfect block drives him back, and at the wall he is stunned 1 s | **Iron Wall**: the perfect-block window doubles and each one is a free slot hit, *but you cannot dodge* |
| **Blood** | slow, cruel, certain | **Serrated Edge**: slot hits bleed 1; **Open Vein**: a bleeding foe's tells are 20% slower; **Bloodlust**: each bleed tick gives you stamina | **Haemorrhage**: when he falls, his bleed jumps to the next foe, *but your bare hits do nothing* |
| **The Showman** | win them over, then win | **Flourish**: a twirl of the sword (a fast circle) fills the crowd; **Bread and Circuses**: a full meter throws you a heart; **Crowd Pleaser**: at a full meter your next slot hit does 3x | **Darling of Rome**: the meter never drains, and at full he hesitates in awe, *but the crowd's gold is halved* |
| **The Dancer** | never be there | **Blur** (kept); **Sidestep**: a dodge in his tell makes him overcommit, and he is winded; **Matador**: a dodged lunge or pounce sends him into the wall | **Untouchable**: dodges cost nothing, and each one in his tell opens every slot for 1 s, *but you have no shield* |
| **The Brute** | through, not around | **Heavy Arm** (kept); **Plate Breaker**: an overhead strips the plate it hits; **Juggernaut**: lunge through his blow, so your plate takes it and you keep coming | **Titan**: +2 hearts, and every overhead staggers him, *but you cannot dodge or step back* |
| **The Trickster** | make him guess | **Your own feint**: pull a lunge back before it lands and he raises his guard, so the slot opposite opens for 0.6 s; **Mind Reader**: a feint read gives 2 Momentum; **Bait**: lower your shield and his next blow has twice the tell | **Puppeteer**: every third blow of his is a feint you can see coming, *but your hearts are capped at 2* |

- **Each family has about 6 cards,** so about 36 in all, written in the build within these lines.
- **The Trickster's feint is a new gesture** (a lunge pulled back), so it is the riskiest card. It is built first and judged in the card lab (K5).

### K3. Finishers: spend Momentum

- **Execute** (3 Momentum): the next slot hit fells him outright if he is below half.
- **Whirlwind** (2): one slash hits every slot it crosses.
- **Second Breath** (2): refill all your stamina.
- **Disarm** (4): his weapon flies, and he fights barehanded for 3 s.

Finishers come in the draft like any card. A finisher fires with a long press on the sword thumb when you have the pips, so there is no new button.

### K4. Duos: when two families meet

- If you hold at least one card from each of two families, the draft may deal that pair's **duo**: one card that marries them. (Hades does this with its gods.)
- There are 8 duos at first, for example:
  - **Blood Sport** (Blood and Showman): each bleed tick fills the crowd;
  - **Matador's Cape** (Dancer and Wall): a perfect block or a dodge in his tell both give Momentum, and the next one stuns;
  - **Butcher** (Brute and Blood): a stripped plate leaves a bleeding slot;
  - **Sucker Punch** (Trickster and Brute): your own feint followed by an overhead always staggers.
- **A duo shows both families' icons and a gold border.** It is the "I made a build" moment.

### K5. How the deck deals, so a build can form in one run

- **More picks:** a run has 5 picks.
  - **The start card:** your background deals one card from its family. A soldier gets the Wall, a horseman the Dancer, a baker the Showman, a farmer the Brute, a scribe the Trickster and a sailor Blood. This replaces the flat v1 background perks.
  - **Plus a draft after each of the four fells.**
- **Pull:** each draft weights families you already hold, as with Hades' gods, so builds deepen.
  - Three cards, at most two from the same family.
  - One is priced in gold (D), and it is the slot where duos and keystones usually appear.
- **The old "+20%" commons leave the draft.** They move to the armourer's tent as cheap wares (D), so the draft is always a choice between rules.
- **The ludus opens families** (E): a new save starts with the Wall, the Dancer and the Brute. Blood, the Showman and the Trickster are unlocked with fame at the barracks. The tailor and the shrine stay as in E.
- **The card lab (dev channel only):** a TUNE page to pick any cards before a bout, so the designer can try combos without waiting for the deal.

### K6. Guard rails

- **Every card is one line,** at most about 60 characters, with its numbers in TUNING.
- **A keystone always names its price on the card.**
- **No card makes a bout winnable without reading his tells.** The naked run (v0.2 H) still holds: a plain fighter with no cards can fell the Pit champion.
- **The draft card has a "?"** that shows how a duo works and which families you hold, so synergy is never hidden.

## G. Saves

`saveVersion` bump. `migrate` keeps the rival, the pit wall, the feint lesson and the bouts seen, and adds:
- the arenas opened (a v1 save that felled a champion opens the City);
- fame (a v1 champion felled gives its 10, and so does each won run on the wall);
- the ludus unlocks;
- the Wall of Champions (the v1 wins become its first entries);
- the veteran roster.

The run resume stays at bout start, now with the arena and the gold.

- animated art beyond pinned sprites;
- voice;
- a market screen between every bout.

## J. Acceptance

- One run is one arena, 3 to 6 minutes.
- Each arena's twist reads in its first bout without a tutorial longer than one caption.
- The crowd meter, gold card, tent and ludus never overlap at 667x375, 844x390 or 932x430.

- A v1 save migrates with its wins on the Wall and its fame.

## Step 2 (locked 2026-10-14): cards become gear, cascading deals, and gear that covers gaps

**What the designer said (2026-10-14):**
- "Could we turn the cards into gear that has an aesthetic impact?" Then: "Yes, I agree", to one piece per slot and finishers as war paint.
- After a won run: "I was almost always going for the neck gap. While the shielding covers that sometimes, maybe it's actually good for some people or some gear to block certain gaps, so I have to learn to hit others."
- A Candlesan video on loot tables (`docs/pattern-library.md` L9, cascading rolls).

**Step 2 is one Opus build, reviewed. It changes the look and the deal, not the fight's base rules.**

### G1. Every card is a piece of gear

- **Each of the 24 family cards and Unbreakable gets:**
  - a slot: helmet, shield, weapon, chest, arms, legs, cloak or belt;
  - a gear name;
  - a procedural look.
  
  The effect lines stay as they are. Example names: Thorns becomes a spiked bronze shield, Serrated Edge a saw-toothed blade, Sidestep dancer's sandals, Bloodlust a wolf pelt, Juggernaut iron pauldrons.
- **A family look,** so a build reads at a glance:

  | Family | Look |
  | --- | --- |
  | The Wall | bronze and big shields |
  | Blood | dark red, barbs and hooks |
  | The Dancer | light cloth and ribbons |
  | The Brute | black iron and spikes |

- **Keystones are signature pieces:**
  - **Iron Wall:** a tower shield.
  - **Untouchable:** a cape and no shield, visibly.
  - **Titan:** full black plate.
  - **Haemorrhage:** a hooked sica.
- **Rarity has a colour:** common, rare, epic, legendary. It shows on the card's border and as a faint glint on the worn piece.
- **One piece per slot.** Picking a piece for a filled slot shows "replaces Bronze Helm" on the card, and the old piece is gone.
- **Gear never adds plates.** Titan's black plate is a look; its line (+2 hearts, staggering overheads, no dodge) is unchanged.
- **Drawn by the game,** on both fighters, layered on v1's painted parts. The slot names are the file names for the painted gear pack later (F), with the procedural look kept as the fallback.

### G2. Duos are set bonuses

- A duo card is now a **set bonus**, offered when you wear at least one piece of each of its two families.
- It is shown as a set name on the card, for example "Matador's Cape set: ...", and as a small set mark on the fighter card. It takes no slot.

### G3. Finishers are war paint

- The four finishers are war paint that Old Brutus paints on your face and sword arm: a pattern per finisher.
- They are dealt and fired as now (the long press). They take no slot.

### G4. The deal cascades (L9)

- **The priced slot rolls top-down,** with fixed chances in TUNING:
  1. a set bonus, if you qualify;
  2. a legendary (a keystone);
  3. an epic;
  4. otherwise a rare.
  
  A tier with nothing left to deal falls through to the next.
- **The free slots keep their grade-based tier roll.**
- **Within any tier, the family is rolled before the piece.** Families you wear are weighted up, then a piece is picked inside the family. Adding pieces to one family never changes another family's share.
- **The "?" card shows the priced slot's odds,** for example "Set 60% · Legendary 15% · Epic 30%", and the families you wear.

### G5. His gear covers gaps (the neck lesson)

- **Opponents wear gear from the same deck,** with more pieces in later bouts, and legendary pieces for champions. His gear is a tell: a spiked shield means don't hammer his guard, a wolf pelt means he drinks blood. A foe's piece gives him its effect only where the effect makes sense for the AI. Otherwise it is a look and a warning (simplest option; the builder lists which).
- **Gap guards,** a new small set of foe-only pieces. Each covers one joint gap with a visible guard, so that gap is shut for the bout:
  - a **gorget** covers the neck;
  - a **manica** (an arm guard) covers the armpit;
  - a **wide belt** covers the waist;
  - **couters** cover the elbow;
  - **knee guards** cover the knee.
- **His intro card shows which gaps are guarded.**
- **The rules:**
  - at least 2 gaps are always open;
  - the bout 1 sparring partner never wears a guard, so the neck lesson still teaches;
  - from bout 2 on, each foe has a chance of one or two guards, rising by bout and set in TUNING;
  - the gorget is the most common guard, so a neck-only player meets it often and has to learn the armpit, waist, elbow and knee;
  - the champion always wears at least one guard.
- **The rival remembers,** in the spirit of v1's memory. The rival is the champion who beat you. Next time, he wears a guard on the gap you hit most against him.

### G6. Saves

- `saveVersion` 6.
- **The picks keep their card ids,** so a saved run migrates as is. The gear is derived from the ids.

### G7. Acceptance

- Every piece draws on both fighters at 667x375, 844x390 and 932x430. The slot replace, the set mark and the war paint show.
- The cascade's odds match its TUNING over a seeded sample (a sample only, not a search).
- At least 2 gaps are always open, and bout 1 is never guarded.
- `npm run smoke` passes.
- Frame time is no worse than arena-v18.
- **The designer's runs judge the rest.**

### F1. Five sizes, and no two the same

- **Five bands for you and for him.** The numbers are starting points in TUNING:

  | Band | Scale | Health | Reach | Damage | Wind-up | Feet |

  | Runt | 0.75 | 0.65x | -0.08 | 0.8x | 0.8x (faster) | 0.65x (quicker) |

- **The points split by a rolled profile,** with the shares in TUNING:
  - **Brute:** high strength, high stamina, low speed.
  - **Duelist:** high speed, fair strength and stamina.
  - **Glass cannon:** high strength, high speed, low stamina.
  - **Tank:** high stamina, fair strength, low speed.
  - **Balanced.**
- **Size leans the profile roll.** A Giant is more often a Brute or a Tank, and a Runt a Duelist or a Glass cannon. Any profile is still possible at any size.
- **Every foe keeps at least one stat at 2 or below until the champion,** and the champion keeps one at 3 or below, so every fight has a weakness to read.

  - "Balanced: no easy opening."

### F3. Your wheels reshape each other (pattern L8)

- **Your band tilts your stat wheels.** A Giant's strength wheel grows its strong slices and its speed wheel shrinks its fast ones. A Runt is the reverse.
- **Each stat result reshapes the next wheel.** A Strong or Mighty result shrinks the next stat wheel's top slices by one tier step, and a Feeble or Fair result grows them. All lucky is still possible, just rare.
- **Before each spin, the slices visibly grow and shrink from the last result,** so you see the cause before the effect.
- **The tier letter (S to D)** still sums the stats, and the underdog bonus stays.

### F4. Better-looking wheels

The roll scene gets a real wheel-of-fate look, drawn by the game:
- **The wheel itself:**
  - a rim with gold studs;
  - pegs between slices, with a ticker that flicks on each peg (a tick sound per peg, pitch rising as it slows);
  - slices with a gentle gradient, sized by their odds;
  - a hub with the wheel's question ("Size?", "Strength?").
- **The spin:** a fast start, then an eased slowdown with a last-peg tease that wobbles before it settles.
- **The landing:**
  - a flash on the slice;
  - the slice pops out a little;
  - the answer drops in big below ("MIGHTY!");
  - rare slivers (Runt, Giant, Mighty, Feeble) get a bigger burst and a crowd "ooh".
- **Progress pips** for the eight wheels.
- **The fighter card builds up beside the wheels** as each answer lands.
- **Tap to spin, tap again to snap,** as now. Each wheel stays about 2 s.

### F5. More juice in the fight

Principle 16: every action sounds and shows.

| Moment | Juice |
| --- | --- |
| a gap (slot) hit | a short hit-stop (about 60 ms), a bright spark at the gap, a deeper hit sound |
| a fell | a slow-motion second, the camera punching in a little, the crowd roar, dust as he drops |
| a perfect block | a white ring flash on the shield, a sharp ring sound, a tiny shake |
| a feint read | a violet shimmer bursting off him, a crowd "ooh" |
| a lunge | a dust puff at the back foot, a whoosh |
| a dodge | a short ghost trail, a sand scuff |
| a finisher | the war paint glowing, a beat of slow motion, a named banner ("EXECUTE!") |
| his wind-up | a creak or grunt by weapon, so the tell is heard as well as seen |
| a Giant's step | a soft thud and a tiny shake |
| getting hit | a red edge flash, your fighter recoiling, a heart-shatter pop on the HUD |

- **Comfort and settings:** effects respect mute. Shake is small, and off when the system asks for reduced motion if the engine exposes it (otherwise a TUNING switch).
- **All numbers** are in `TUNING.juice`.
- **No gore:** sparks, sand and straw only.

## Step 8 (locked 2026-10-24): a grade that counts what you did well

### Loading, handled gracefully (added 2026-11-03)

**The designer:** "Sometimes images take a second to load; I think we need to handle that a bit more gracefully." - **Failures:** an image that fails to load keeps the drawn look, with no console error and no retry loop.

## Step 21 (locked 2026-11-07): each weapon hits with its striking part, in its own way, and shows it

**The designer:** "The hitbox of the axe was a bit weird; it still needed to jab, so maybe it should have a spike." Then: "Not just the spike: we should rethink the way different weapons hit." And: "Maybe that means when picking weapons we should show which parts of the weapon hit."

Today every weapon hits the same way:
- a jab lands at the tip, a slash anywhere along the weapon;
- weapons differ only in reach, speed, damage and a slash multiplier.

This step replaces that with one rule set, for you and for every foe. Each weapon keeps its reach, speed and damage numbers; its `slash` multiplier gives way to the rules below.

**1. Only the striking part deals the real hit.**
- Each weapon's painting has striking zones, measured in px on its Pack 23 painting (`TUNING.strike.<weapon>`).
- A blow that connects with the haft or shaft is a **thud**: small damage (`thud`, about 25%) and a little push, with no gap bonus, no bleed and no dent. It has a dull sound and the word "THUD".
- A clean hit flashes the striking part.

**2. Three kinds of hit,** built on systems already in the game:

| Kind | Strong against | Weak against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cut** (an edge drawn across) | bare skin: causes bleed | plate: glances off for little |
| **Pierce** (a point driven in) | gaps: the gap bonus | broad plate |
| **Crush** (a heavy head) | plate: dents and breaks it, staggers | no bleed |

**3. Each weapon's zones:**

| Weapon | Zones | Jab | Slash and overhead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gladius | point: pierce; both edges: cut | pierce | cut |
| Dagger | point: pierce; short edges: cut | fast pierce | short cut |
| Spear | leaf head only: pierce (a slash with the head: a light cut) | long pierce | shaft thud, unless the head connects |
| Trident | three prongs: pierce, with a wider point zone that finds gaps more easily | pierce | shaft thud, unless the prongs connect |
| Sica | inner curve: hooking cut (more bleed, and a small pull in) | the back of the curve: weak | cut |
| Axe | side blade: cut and crush; top spike (Pack 28): pierce | short pierce with the spike | cut and crush with the blade, haft thud |
| Mace | ball, all round: crush | crush | crush, haft thud |
| Fists | knuckles: crush (light: staggers, never dents) | crush | crush |

The numbers go in `TUNING.strike`. Cards that boost bleed, gaps or dents now read through these kinds, with the same effects.

**4. Showing it when picking:**
- In the armourer's tent's weapon rack and on the inventory's Weapon slot, each weapon is drawn with its striking zones lit in a colour per kind: cut red along the edge, pierce gold at the point, crush steel-blue on the head. The haft is left dim.
- Under it go one or two words, for example "Cut · Pierce" or "Crush".
- The "?" page gets a small key of the three colours.
- No sentences.

### Acceptance

- Scripted checks for each weapon:
  - jab and slash at its striking part, and at its haft (a thud);
  - each kind against bare skin, a gap and a plate.
- A foe's shaft hit on you is a thud.

  - the rack with every weapon's zones;
