# Concept: Checkpoint

- **Slug:** checkpoint
- **Pattern:** P10 (spot the thing under time pressure)
- **Date:** 2026-09-30
- **Status:** concept, gate passed

## One sentence

X-ray bags roll down the belt; tap the knife, the lighter, the too-big bottle before the bag leaves the screen, and wave the innocent ones through without a false alarm.

## The first ten seconds

A dark blue X-ray screen fills the phone in portrait. A bag slides in from the top on a belt, its contents drawn as glowing outlines: a shirt, a phone, headphones, and a pair of scissors. You tap the scissors: a red ring, a "clack", +100, the bag slides off to the side. The next bag has nothing wrong; you leave it and it rolls out the bottom with a soft chime, +20. The third has a gun shape hidden under a laptop.

## The fantasy

You are the officer who never misses. Mastery is reading a bag in half a second, seeing the shape of the forbidden thing through the clutter, and never stopping an innocent bag.

## Three tips

1. Scan for the outline, not the colour: every contraband item has a telling silhouette (a blade's point, a lighter's hood).
2. Check the dense corner first: items overlap there and that is where things hide.
3. Leave a clean bag alone: a false alarm costs more than a slow clean bag.

## Perfect versus sloppy

A perfect shift catches every item with the belt at full speed and no false alarms: the streak climbs to x4 and the belt speeds up with a rising hum. A sloppy shift taps a harmless hairdryer (a buzzer, a time penalty, the streak breaks) and lets a knife through (a red flash at the bottom, a strike).

## What it borrows

"Papers, Please" and the Flash-era airport scanner games: the tension of reading clutter against the clock, and the specific pleasure of a clean catch.

## Guessed kernel

Verb: tap an item in a moving bag. Skill axis: shape recognition speed under clutter. Content: seeded bags from an item table; ten shifts of rising belt speed and clutter; three strikes end a shift.
